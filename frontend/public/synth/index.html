FILAMENT

Cathode, the second device in the rack: one voice, two VCOs, a screaming high-pass and low-pass pair, and a patch panel. Everything is pre-wired; a cable overrides the normal printed under a jack.

A tube synthesizer. The tubes do real work: one drives the filters, one sits inside the Valve filter's resonance loop, and a push-pull pair runs the output. Play it with the keys below, your computer keyboard, or a MIDI keyboard.

Dekatron, a counting-tube drum machine: kick, snare, hats, clap, tom and rim, synthesized, with a 16-step pattern. The keys play the voices. Swing, accents and the pattern length live on the panel.

Lattice, a wavetable instrument: two morphing oscillators, warp, unison, a sub and noise, two filters, and a modulation matrix you drag into place. Draw LFO shapes, or drop a WAV file to play it as a cycle.

Thyratron, a monophonic ladder synthesizer: three oscillators, a mixer with noise and feedback, and a 24 dB/octave ladder low-pass that will sing when the emphasis is full. Glide, two contours, and the pitch and mod wheels.

Gleam, a touch field. Each finger is a voice: across is pitch, up is timbre, pressure is level. Hold latches that point, or a MIDI note, after you let go. A generator can walk, orbit, drift, or arpeggiate the scale while you play on top. Panic, or hold again, lets the latch go. A room view places sounding orbs around a listener, and the same pad can sweep the rack master.

Mote, a granular instrument: load any sound, record the microphone, or capture the rack, then play it as a cloud of grains. Capture rack is an input, not an export. Drag the highlighted region to move through the waveform. The red rec button saves what you hear as a WAV.

Heptode plays a MIDI file, then lets you reshape it. Every channel starts as the same plain voice. Morph the wave, open the filter, and drag each track around the stereo field. The red rec button saves what you hear as a WAV.

Geissler is a small meadow. Reeds hum, grazers pluck, stalks hit, and flickers chirp. They meet, eat, breed and die, and each event is a note. Tap the field to drop food or spawn a creature. Drag to herd them. The red rec button saves what you hear as a WAV.

Pentagrid is a shortwave receiver in the rack. Drag the dial across a made-up band: noise, crackle, fading, whistles, off-tune formant speech, Morse, an FSK warble, chimes, and time pips. Play mode turns the keys into those beats. The red rec button saves what you hear as a WAV.

Orthicon paints a picture of sound. Time runs across, frequency runs up, and a playhead scans it back into audio. Paint with a brush, drop a PNG or JPG, or analyse a recording. Capture rack is an input, not an export. The red rec button saves what you hear as a WAV.

Trochotron grows a piece from a seed. Pick a walk (an L-system, a Mandelbrot or Julia orbit, the logistic map, Lorenz, or a digit sequence), then play. The same seed and the same settings are the same piece. Drag the picture to change it. Copy the link, or export a .mid. The red rec button saves what you hear as a WAV.

Gyrotron is a gravity sequencer. Bodies orbit wells the way electrons spiral in a cavity. A collision or a crossing of an orbit rings a note in the scale you chose. Touch places a well or a body, and a drag flings one. The red rec button saves what you hear as a WAV.

Klystron is a talk box. A carrier synth is shaped by a mouth: a vowel you drag on the pad, a recording, or the microphone (the browser asks permission). A filter bank does the speaking, and a grain cloud can smear or freeze it. The red rec button saves what you hear as a WAV.

Phantastron is a circuit-bent toy. A cheap talking-toy voice runs on a clock you can drag a wire into. Touch a pad and pull a wire to another: clock speed, bit crush, a stuck loop, or feedback. The same bend is the same glitch. The red rec button saves what you hear as a WAV.

Additron is an additive organ. Draw the harmonics, or pull the stops, and morph between two spectra. Each partial keeps its own decay. A rotary speaker throws the sound around the room. The red rec button saves what you hear as a WAV.

Rhumbatron is a cavity you can touch: a string, a bore, or a plate. Drag the bright point to move where the energy enters. Pluck it, strike it, bow it, or blow it. The keys and a MIDI keyboard play the same point. Panic lets every voice go. The red rec button saves what you hear as a WAV.

Resnatron is a patch bay of cavities. Nothing is pre-wired. Drag a gold jack onto a silver one, or tap one then the other. Starter patches are saved cables. Copy link shares the bay. The red rec button saves what you hear as a WAV.

Each letter is a device that is already loaded. Level, pan, mute and solo sit in front of the shared ensemble. Centre pan, full level and zero sends leave the sound alone. Arm one device and the keys and MIDI play that device. Arm several and they play together. With no arm lit, the keys follow the device lit on the rail. Reverb and delay are shared sends; the returns join the mix, so the red record button writes one WAV of everything you hear. Save mix keeps this desk in the browser. Playing it. Click or touch the keys (lower on a key is louder). Computer keyboard: A W S E D F T G Y H U J K play an octave, Z X shift the octave, C V set velocity, space is the sustain pedal. Panic silences every note. Knobs: drag up or down (shift for fine), arrow keys, double click to reset. A MIDI keyboard works in Chrome, Edge and Firefox after you press Connect MIDI. Safari has no Web MIDI, so on an iPhone or a Mac in Safari use the keys on screen.

MIDI files. Press rec and play: the first note starts the take, on whichever device the rail has lit. Play it back, or download it as a .mid. Import a .mid (or drop one on the strip) and pick Filament, Cathode or off for each track; both devices play at once. Render wav writes the file (or the take) through both devices and the rack, up to 10 minutes. Files stay in your browser; nothing is uploaded.

Song library. Open songs and pick a tune, or drop a .mid file on that strip. Play and stop are the rack transport, and the tempo box (Rack tab) sets the speed. Each MIDI channel can go to Filament, Cathode, Dekatron, or off. Channel 10 starts on Dekatron. The bundled tunes are public domain, or original demos written for this page. Songs in the Halloween set load a matching Filament preset and a matching Cathode preset. Mountain King, Cellar Stairs and Porch Ghost also load a Dekatron pattern, and their hat part plays on Dekatron.

Sculpt. A hand instrument on its own page: pinch a glowing orb and move it in the space in front of you. The camera picture stays in the browser.

Privacy. No ads, no cookies, no accounts. Your patches and settings stay in this browser. DXEdge counts visits anonymously, the same way as the rest of the site. Tip the developer: helps cover hosting and development.

Filament v0.19: Filament, Cathode, Thyratron, Dekatron, Lattice, Gleam, Mote, Heptode, Geissler, Pentagrid, Orthicon, Trochotron, Gyrotron, Klystron, Phantastron, Additron, Rhumbatron, and Resnatron in one rack, with a mixer, a master clock, project files, a song library, a MIDI deck, and a master delay, reverb and compressor. Gleam is the touch field; full screen uses the same voices. Heptode plays MIDI files. Geissler is a meadow of reeds, grazers, stalks and flickers. Pentagrid is the shortwave dial. Orthicon paints a spectrogram and scans it back into sound. Trochotron grows a piece from a seed. Gyrotron is a gravity sequencer: bodies orbit wells and ring a note when they collide or cross. Klystron is a talk box: a carrier shaped by a vowel, a file, or the microphone, then smeared by grains. Phantastron is a circuit-bent toy. Additron is an additive organ: draw the harmonics and a rotor throws them around the room. Rhumbatron is a string, a bore, or a plate you can strike. Resnatron is a patch bay of cavities. Drag a gold jack onto a silver one, or share the bay with a link. The red rec button records a 24-bit WAV of the master on every device. The file is named for the lit device, or rack when several are playing. The Master panel, on the Rack tab below the instrument, has the same recording plus the effects and render wav. Two more sit on their own pages: Hexode, six-operator FM, and Anode, an acid bass groovebox. Part of DXEdge · tube voicings shared with the Old Time Radio rack.
